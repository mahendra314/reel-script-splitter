Most people scroll past a video in the first two seconds. So your opening line has one job: give a reason to stay.

Start with the result, not the setup. Say what the viewer will get if they watch to the end.

Keep your sentences short. A short sentence is easy to hear, easy to read on screen, and easy to follow at speed.

Cut every pause you do not need. Silence is fine when it creates tension, but dead air usually makes people leave.

Use one idea per clip. If you try to teach three things in fifteen seconds, none of them will land.

End with a clear next step. Tell the viewer what to do: follow for part two, save this for later, or comment your answer.

Test your script out loud before you record it. If a sentence is hard to say in one breath, split it into two.
